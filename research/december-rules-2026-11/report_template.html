Infinity rules for December: E8 at Stage 1, proof of edge at Stages 2 and 3

Every real-rules trade on IPFX was replayed at E8 Markets prices and costs, every trader was run through E8 One's challenge rules, and {{N_RULESETS}} candidate Stage 2 and 3 rule sets were simulated on the real shape of our traders' results. Stage 1 can copy E8 exactly, and that tells us who can follow E8's risk rules. It does not tell us who has an edge: the 7 E8 passes in our data all came from one or two very large wins on very tight stops, and none of those traders kept making money afterwards. Stages 2 and 3 therefore have to do the real filtering, using enough trades that luck cannot carry a trader through, and Stage 3 has to repeat the result on fresh trades before anyone is paid.

Evidence 1: our traders replayed on E8

Each closed trade on a real-rules account (Infinity and Traditional, {{N_TRADES}} trades from {{N_TRADERS}} traders, 19 July to 25 November) was re-priced at E8: IPFX's spread replaced by E8's average spread for that symbol from about 30,000 side-by-side samples per major pair, E8's published commission instead of IPFX's, and extra slippage per side. Each trade keeps the same size as a share of the account, so a trader risking 0.4% on IPFX risks 0.4% on E8. Floating losses use each trade's recorded worst point, with overlapping trades assumed to hit their worst at the same time.

{{TABLE_E8}}

Changing E8's day boundary to CET midnight, or ignoring floating losses, leaves the result at 7 passes. Most attempts do not finish: at about 0.3% to 0.4% risk a trade, 6% is 15 to 20 clear winning trades away, and most traders stopped long before.

Evidence 2: is anyone's edge real?

{{CHART_HIST}}
  • Of 74 traders with 20 or more trades, 7 have a positive average at E8 costs, and all but one are only just above zero.
  • If nobody had any edge, chance alone would give about 2 traders a score above 2. We see 1. Nobody scores above 3.
  • Traders who were profitable in the first half of their trades averaged −0.60R a trade in the second half; only 2 of 19 stayed positive.
  • What does persist is how badly losing traders lose. That is why the current rules already stop almost all of them.
  • Costs matter by market: at E8, gold costs about 0.08R a trade more than at IPFX, euro and yen pairs 0.18R to 0.29R, Swiss franc 0.45R, and Bitcoin about 12R because of its spread against tight stops.

Limits of this work

  • The data is small: the median trader has 5 trades, only 18 have 50 or more, and nobody has traded a Stage 2 account. Individual edge cannot be proven from this history, so the Stage 2 and 3 numbers come from simulation on our traders' real result shapes.
  • E8 costs are estimates: average spreads from our own side-by-side samples and E8's published commission. Real E8 fills, swaps and requotes were not available. Three cost levels were tested and the conclusions hold for all three.
  • Traders traded under IPFX's rules, not E8's. Some would have behaved differently on E8.
  • E8 One's official help centre preset is a 6% target, 3% daily loss and 4% dynamic drawdown. The E8 website's $100K configurator shows 9%, 4% and 6%. The help centre preset is used for Stage 1.
