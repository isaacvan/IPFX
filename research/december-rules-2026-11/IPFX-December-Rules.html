Infinity rules for December: E8 at Stage 1, proof of edge at Stages 2 and 3

Every real-rules trade on IPFX was replayed at E8 Markets prices and costs, every trader was run through E8 One's challenge rules, and 129 candidate Stage 2 and 3 rule sets were simulated on the real shape of our traders' results. This page gives the recommended rules and the evidence behind each number.

Stage 1 can copy E8 exactly, and that tells us who can follow E8's risk rules. It does not tell us who has an edge: the 7 E8 passes in our data all came from one or two very large wins on very tight stops, and none of those traders kept making money afterwards. Stages 2 and 3 therefore have to do the real filtering, using enough trades that luck cannot carry a trader through, and Stage 3 has to repeat the result on fresh trades before anyone is paid.

Recommended rules

Stage 1 · free · $10,000 simulated

E8 One, exactly

  • Profit target 6% of closed profit
  • Daily loss 3% of the starting balance, measured from each day's starting balance, floating losses included. Hard breach.
  • Dynamic drawdown 4% below the highest closed balance. It only rises when profit is closed, and stops rising once it reaches the starting balance.
  • No minimum days, no consistency rule, no score, no minimum trades
  • News and weekend holding allowed. Leverage up to 1:30 on FX, 1:15 on indices and metals.
  • Not allowed: putting the whole daily limit on one trade, and hedging between accounts
  • At least one closed trade every 60 days

Matches the E8 One preset in E8's help centre, so a pass means the trader stayed inside E8's risk rules. In our data that alone does not show edge: 20% to 55% of simulated Stage 1 passers have none.

Stage 2 · $5,000 simulated

Show your edge

  • Profit target 6%, risk up to 0.5% per counted trade
  • Maximum drawdown 8% of the starting balance (fixed), daily loss 3%
  • At least 40 counted trades over at least 10 trading days
  • Consistency score at least 1.5 on Stage 2 trades (each trade capped at +5R, as now)
  • Best day no more than 40% of profit; biggest 3 winning trades no more than 50% of profit
  • Profit share held, as now. Verify identity to move up.

Lottery wins cannot carry a pass: the +5R cap and the top-3 rule stop one or two huge trades counting as proof. After a Stage 2 pass, simulated no-edge share falls to 0.1% to 5%. This is where IPFX starts copying.

Stage 3 · $10,000 simulated

Prove it again

  • Fresh account: profit target 6%, risk up to 0.5%, drawdown 8% fixed, daily loss 3%
  • At least 60 new counted trades over at least 15 trading days
  • Score at least 1.5 on Stage 3 trades alone, and at least 3.0 on Stage 2 and 3 trades together
  • Best day no more than 35% of profit; biggest 3 winning trades no more than 40% of profit
  • Then pay: 85% of eligible Stage 2 and 3 profit, up to $700, minimum $500 (unchanged)

A combined score of 3.0 over about 100 or more trades means a result that random trading reaches about 1 time in 750. Repeating it on fresh Stage 3 trades rules out a lucky streak in Stage 2.

What changes from today. Stage 1 drops the 20-trade minimum and the score, and uses E8's target and limits instead. Stages 2 and 3 move to 6% targets with a wider fixed 8% drawdown, add minimum trading days and a top-3-trades limit, and Stage 3 must pass on its own trades as well as combined (combined now means Stages 2 and 3 only, not Stage 1). Stage 4 is unchanged. Payout amounts are unchanged.

A disciplined trader with a +0.2R edge needs about 230 counted trades to be paid (about 3 months at the pace of our active traders); a +0.3R trader about 136. For disciplined traders the recommended rules cut paid zero-edge traders from 2.9% to 0.7%, pay fewer traders with a tiny edge (+0.05R to +0.1R), and pay slightly more at +0.2R and above. For traders whose profits come in rare big wins (our traders' shape) they are stricter: a +0.3R trader of that kind is paid 10% of the time instead of 19%, because big wins count for less. That is the price of not paying lucky streaks. 129 rule sets were compared; drawdown size, target and trade minimums changed results little, the combined score threshold mattered most.

Does paying at Stage 3 make IPFX money?

IPFX makes money from a paid trader by copying their trades onto E8 accounts. If their edge is real, that is worth far more than the payout. The question is how many paid traders have no edge.   • Do not copy at full size after Stage 1. In simulation 20% (disciplined) to 55% (our traders' shape) of Stage 1 passers have no edge, and the 7 real E8 passes did not hold up. Start copying after a Stage 2 pass, when the no-edge share is 0.1% to 5%.

  • Expect very few payouts at first. At today's activity about 2 traders a month would pass Stage 1, and a Stage 3 payout needs 100 or more counted trades after that.

Evidence 1: our traders replayed on E8

Each closed trade on a real-rules account (Infinity and Traditional, 5,238 trades from 485 traders, 19 July to 25 November) was re-priced at E8: IPFX's spread replaced by E8's average spread for that symbol from about 30,000 side-by-side samples per major pair, E8's published commission instead of IPFX's, and extra slippage per side. Each trade keeps the same size as a share of the account, so a trader risking 0.4% on IPFX risks 0.4% on E8. Evidence 2: is anyone's edge real?

  • Of 74 traders with 20 or more trades, 7 have a positive average at E8 costs, and all but one are only just above zero.
  • If nobody had any edge, chance alone would give about 2 traders a score above 2. We see 1. Nobody scores above 3.
  • Traders who were profitable in the first half of their trades averaged −0.60R a trade in the second half; only 2 of 19 stayed positive.
  • What does persist is how badly losing traders lose. That is why the current rules already stop almost all of them.
  • Costs matter by market: at E8, gold costs about 0.08R a trade more than at IPFX, euro and yen pairs 0.18R to 0.29R, Swiss franc 0.45R, and Bitcoin about 12R because of its spread against tight stops.

Limits of this work

  • The data is small: the median trader has 5 trades, only 18 have 50 or more, and nobody has traded a Stage 2 account. Individual edge cannot be proven from this history, so the Stage 2 and 3 numbers come from simulation on our traders' real result shapes.
  • E8 costs are estimates: average spreads from our own side-by-side samples and E8's published commission. Real E8 fills, swaps and requotes were not available. Three cost levels were tested and the conclusions hold for all three.
  • Traders traded under IPFX's rules, not E8's. Some would have behaved differently on E8.
  • E8 One's official help centre preset is a 6% target, 3% daily loss and 4% dynamic drawdown. The E8 website's $100K configurator shows 9%, 4% and 6%. The help centre preset is used for Stage 1.
